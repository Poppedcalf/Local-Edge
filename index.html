01 The customer journey

Growth is rarely one website, one post or one campaign.

A potential customer needs to discover a business, understand why it may be right for them, feel confident enough to enquire, and receive a useful response. Local Edge looks at how those steps connect.

  1. 01

    Get discovered

    Understand where the right prospects spend time and how they currently find businesses like yours.

  2. 02

    Make the value clear

    Present the offer, experience, pricing context and practical details in language customers understand.

  3. 03

    Build confidence

    Answer common questions, reduce uncertainty and make important information easy to verify.

  4. 04

    Make action easy

    Give people a clear way to enquire, request a quote or take the next agreed step.

The important bit: More visibility does not automatically mean more sales. The offer, market demand, pricing, service quality and how enquiries are handled all matter. We work on the parts that can be understood and improved.

04 The Local Edge Standard

A standard built around useful, responsible work.

Good work is not just a polished deliverable. It starts with understanding the problem, making sensible decisions, communicating clearly and being honest about what can—and cannot—be controlled.

  • Research before recommendations

    Understand the business, customer, market and current process before deciding what to change.

  • Clarity over complexity

    Prioritise actions that fit the objective, budget, team and capabilities of the business.

  • Useful digital foundations

    Build for real visitors: mobile usability, clear information, accessible contact routes and sensible performance.

  • Respectful outreach

    Use relevant, personalised communication. Avoid spam, misleading claims and pressure tactics.

  • Transparent scope

    Define deliverables, exclusions, responsibilities, ownership and fees before paid work starts.

  • Evidence before claims

    Track the activity and outcomes that can be observed. Never invent testimonials, statistics or guaranteed results.

08 How we work together

A clear process from understanding to improvement.

The exact steps depend on the engagement, but the working principles remain the same: understand, agree, act, review and learn.

  1. 1

    Understand

    Learn about the business, its customers, offer, current channels and immediate challenge.

    We clarify: the outcome that matters.

  2. 2

    Research

    Review relevant information, customer questions, competitors and the existing journey.

    We identify: opportunities and constraints.

  3. 3

    Prioritise

    Choose the most useful work for the budget, timeline and team's capacity.

    You receive: a clear proposed scope.

  4. 4

    Build or activate

    Create the agreed digital assets, research, outreach materials or customer pathways.

    We agree: who is responsible for each action.

  5. 5

    Track

    Record activity, responses, enquiries and other agreed indicators without overstating results.

    You can see: what has actually happened.

  6. 6

    Learn & improve

    Review what worked, what did not and what should change next.

    We decide: the next sensible step.

Commercial terms: Fees, payment milestones, commissions where applicable, deliverables and reporting are agreed in writing for each engagement before work starts. Website projects use the published payment structure unless the quotation states otherwise.

12 FAQ

Questions worth asking before we work together.

What does Local Edge actually do?

Local Edge supports market and customer research, digital presence, lead-generation and outreach preparation, and content or campaign support. The right combination depends on the business challenge and is agreed in a written scope. Explore the services.

Can you guarantee more customers or bookings?

No. We can improve specific parts of the customer journey and carry out agreed research or outreach, but customer behaviour and sales depend on market demand, the offer, pricing, competition, service delivery and follow-up. We do not promise a sales result we cannot control.

How much does a website cost?

Starting prices are $125 for Starter, $250 for Business, $350 for Hospitality and $600 for Advanced. The final scope and price are agreed in writing first. Compare packages.

How many revisions are included?

One revision round for Starter and two for Business and Hospitality websites, within the agreed scope. Advanced projects define revisions in the quotation.

How long will it take?

Timelines depend on the project and how quickly content and feedback arrive. The delivery window is agreed before work begins.

What do I need to provide?

Accurate business information and images you are authorised to use. Copywriting, photography or image sourcing is included only when the quotation says so.

Can you build bookings, payments or live availability?

These need a feasibility assessment and separate quotation. Standard packages provide enquiry or booking-request paths, not a live reservation engine or online payment system.

Is the free demo really free?

Yes, as a limited concept preview subject to availability and suitability. It is not a finished production website, and there is no obligation to buy. See how it works.
