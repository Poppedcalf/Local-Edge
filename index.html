01 What a website is for

A website is more than decoration. It has four jobs.

When a visitor lands on your page, they are silently asking four questions. A good website answers each one quickly, on a phone, on a patchy connection.

  1. A

    Understand

    What do you offer, who is it for, and where are you?

  2. B

    Trust

    Does this look real, current and cared for? Are the details consistent?

  3. C

    Explore

    Can I compare options, see what is included and find answers myself?

  4. D

    Contact

    Can I ask a question or request a quote in a moment, without friction?

Local Edge focuses on the parts a website can control—clarity, usability, presentation and the path to contact. Demand, pricing, reputation and how quickly enquiries are answered still depend on the business.

04 The Local Edge Standard

What goes into a properly delivered website

Most of the work in a good website is invisible to the visitor. They simply notice that it is easy to read, quick to open and simple to use. This is the checklist each project is built and checked against.

  • Works on a phone

    Layouts are checked at phone, tablet and desktop widths, with comfortable tap targets and readable text.

  • Light on data

    Images are cropped on purpose, sized for their place and compressed, with no unnecessary libraries.

  • Accessible by design

    Real labels on forms, visible focus, keyboard navigation, good contrast and meaningful image descriptions.

  • Easy to find

    Clear page titles, descriptions, headings and social-sharing details, so links look right when shared.

  • Contact that works

    WhatsApp, call and form links are tested before handover, and nothing pretends to work when it does not.

  • Honest and clear

    No fake reviews, hidden tracking or invented numbers. Scope, ownership and handover are written down.

08 How we work together

Six stages, from first conversation to ongoing support

The aim is a collaborative, organised project. You always know what to provide and what to expect next.

  1. 1

    Understand

    Clarify your audience, current online presence and main business challenge.

    You provide: a short description and current links.

  2. 2

    Define

    Agree deliverables, exclusions, price and timeline in writing.

    You receive: a written scope and quotation.

  3. 3

    Concept

    Where appropriate, review a limited concept—not a finished production website.

    You decide: whether to proceed.

  4. 4

    Build

    Build the agreed pages, content structure, visuals and enquiry pathways.

    You provide: accurate information and authorised images.

  5. 5

    Review

    Review the work within the included revision rounds and send consolidated feedback.

    You provide: one clear set of notes per round.

  6. 6

    Launch & support

    Complete final checks, then publish or hand over as agreed. Optional care can follow.

    You receive: handover details and agreed account ownership.

Payment: 60% to begin and 40% before final publication or handover, subject to written agreement. Timelines start once the deposit, requirements and necessary content have been received. No turnaround is promised until it is agreed for your project.

12 FAQ

Common questions, answered plainly

How much does a website cost?

Starting prices are $125 for Starter, $250 for Business, $350 for Hospitality and $600 for Advanced. The final scope and price are agreed in writing first. Compare packages.

How many revisions are included?

One revision round for Starter and two for Business and Hospitality websites, within the agreed scope. Advanced projects define revisions in the quotation.

How long will it take?

Timelines depend on the project and how quickly content and feedback arrive. The delivery window is agreed before work begins.

What do I need to provide?

Accurate business information and images you are authorised to use. Copywriting, photography or image sourcing is included only when the quotation says so.

Can you build bookings, payments or live availability?

These need a feasibility assessment and separate quotation. Standard packages provide enquiry or booking-request paths, not a live reservation engine or online payment system.

Is the free demo really free?

Yes, as a limited concept preview subject to availability and suitability. It is not a finished production website, and there is no obligation to buy. See how it works.

Ready to talk about your business?

Choose the route that suits you. You will not be pressured, and you will always see the scope and price before paying.
