WhatsApp, Social Media or a Website: What Does a Small Business Actually Need?

There is no universal answer. The right mix depends on your customers, their connectivity, your budget and how your business actually runs.

By Amazing Tembo · Last reviewed October 2026 · 6 min read

Ask three people whether a small business needs a website, and you may hear “yes, obviously”, “no, just use WhatsApp” and “Facebook is enough”. Each answer is right for some businesses and wrong for others. The useful question is not which channel is best, but what job each one does, and which jobs your business needs done.

Customers are not all alike, and no channel is universally best. This article compares the three on their roles, strengths, limits, upkeep and cost, then looks at how they fit together.

Three channels, three different jobs

WhatsApp: conversations

Good at: direct, familiar, personal conversations. A customer can ask a question, get an answer, share a photo and agree details in one place.

Limits: customers need your number first, so strangers rarely find you through it. Information is buried in chat history, so you answer the same questions repeatedly. Everything depends on you replying.

Upkeep and cost: the WhatsApp Business app is free to download, and WhatsApp lists tools such as a business profile, a catalogue, quick replies, greeting and away messages, and labels to organise chats. The real cost is your time to reply, and mobile data.

Social media: discovery and updates

Good at: showing that a business is active, sharing photos and news, and reaching people who already follow you. It is quick to start and costs little to begin.

Limits: you do not control the platform, its rules or how widely your posts are shown. Posts move down the feed, so older information is hard to find. The structure is limited, so a clear list of rooms, prices and policies does not fit well in a feed.

Upkeep and cost: it needs regular posting and replying. Paid promotion is optional and a separate cost.

A website: your reference and home base

Good at: holding complete, stable information at an address you control: what you offer, what is included, where you are, how to ask. Visitors can compare options, and anyone can share a link. A site can also appear in search results.

Limits: it has to be built, it will not attract visitors by itself, and its content goes stale unless someone updates it.

Upkeep and cost: the build, a domain name if you want one (usually renewed each year), and time or a care plan to keep content current.

How they work together

The channels complement one another. Here are three illustrative journeys. They are examples of how it can go, not a claim about how every customer behaves.

  • Discovery to detail to conversation. Someone sees photos on a social page, follows a link to a website page with rooms and rates, and then taps WhatsApp to ask about dates.
  • Existing customer. A returning customer already has your number and messages you directly. A website adds nothing to that journey.
  • Search to call. Someone searches for a service in your town, finds an accurate page or listing, and phones.

What the data does and does not say

Zimbabwe

Internet users, social media and mobile connections

6.54 million internet users 38.4% of the population

In the same dataset, social media is reported as 2.60 million “user identities” (15.3%), and cellular mobile connections as 16.2 million (95.0%).

Limits: the report says social media identities may not represent unique individuals, that some mobile connections are voice and SMS only, and that its latest internet figures may under-represent current realities. These are national figures, and they do not describe your customers.

Source: DataReportal, Digital 2026: Zimbabwe. Reflects data available in October 2025 (end of 2025).

What can be said carefully: in this dataset, a large share of the population was not counted as online, and social media identities were a smaller group still. That does not tell you where your customers are or which channel they prefer. It is a reason to check how your own customers actually find and contact you before choosing, and to keep phone and in-person routes open where your customers rely on them.

Choosing a combination: four situations

These are illustrations of the thinking, not rules.

  • A tradesperson whose work comes mostly by referral. A tidy WhatsApp Business profile may do most of the job. A simple one-page site can later act as a reference to send to people who ask what you do.
  • A lodge whose guests do not know it yet. A website with rooms, location and a clear WhatsApp enquiry path does a job that social posts cannot. Social media then supports it with news and photos.
  • A shop with daily offers and local customers. Social media and WhatsApp may be enough for now, because the information changes daily and a website would go stale.
  • A business that cannot keep anything updated regularly. Fewer channels done well beat many done badly. Choose the one you can maintain.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Where do my customers already look for businesses like mine?
  • How do they prefer to get in touch: call, WhatsApp, in person?
  • How much time each week can I give to answering and updating?
  • Can I keep the details accurate, such as rates, hours and availability?
  • What would help a stranger trust me enough to send a first message?

United States survey

Reviews work across all three channels

96% of 1,026 US adults were open to writing a review

Whichever channels you use, happy customers' feedback is the same kind of trust signal. In this survey, most people were open to leaving one.

Limits: this is a US adult panel, not Zimbabwean customers, and “open to” does not mean “will”. Treat it as a prompt to ask your own customers, not as proof about local behaviour.

Source: BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2025, published 29 January 2025.

What to do next

Write down how your last five customers found you and how they first contacted you. That short list will tell you more than any general advice. If it points to a website, start small and keep it accurate. If it does not, a well-kept WhatsApp profile may be the right investment for now.
