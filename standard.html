The Local Edge Standard

Most of the work in a good website is invisible. Visitors simply notice that it is easy to read, quick to open and simple to use. This page lists what goes into that, and what each check means for the people using your site.

01 The checklist

Five areas, each with a practical purpose

This is the standard each project is built and checked against. It is a list of what the work involves, not a certificate: a check is only reported as done when it has actually been performed, and your handover notes record which ones were.

1 Works on every screen

Responsive layouts tested at representative mobile, tablet and desktop widths.
For visitors: nothing is cut off, tiny or hard to tap, whatever device they use.
Clear hierarchy, readable content, consistent spacing and working navigation.
For visitors: they can scan the page and find what they came for without effort.
Logical internal navigation and genuinely useful page content.
For visitors: every link leads somewhere that helps.

2 Prepared for weak connections

Optimised imagery, sensible file sizes and intentional crops.
For visitors: pages open sooner and use less mobile data, and photos show the right part of the subject.
No unnecessary dependencies, libraries or heavy effects.
For visitors: less to download before the page is usable.
Basic performance testing, with attention to Google's Core Web Vitals where measurement is available.
For visitors: a page that loads, responds and stays steady. See the note on measurement below.

3 Accessible and easy to use

Meaningful alt text for informative images, with decorative images handled appropriately.
For visitors: people using screen readers get the information, and are not slowed by decoration.
Accessible forms: real labels, clear error messages, visible focus and keyboard navigation.
For visitors: everyone can complete an enquiry, with a keyboard or a thumb.
Adequate colour contrast.
For visitors: text stays readable in bright sunlight. The WCAG guideline asks for at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text.

4 Findable and shareable

Appropriate page titles, meta descriptions and heading structure, with canonical and URL decisions where applicable.
For visitors: search results describe your pages accurately, which helps the right people click.
Social-sharing metadata and descriptive links.
For visitors: a link shared on WhatsApp or Facebook shows a proper title and picture.

5 Reliable, honest and handed over properly

Working contact and WhatsApp links, and verified form behaviour.
For visitors: when they try to reach you, it works, or it tells them plainly what to do instead.
GitHub Pages-compatible paths and assets, or the agreed host's equivalent.
For you: the site opens the same way on every page and every refresh.
No fabricated trust signals, hidden functionality or needless data collection.
For visitors: nothing on the page pretends to be what it is not.
Clear handover, scope documentation and client ownership of domain and hosting accounts, wherever practical.
For you: you know what you have, who owns it and how to change it.

02 How performance is measured

Targets, not guarantees

Google publishes three recommended “good” targets for how a page feels to use. Local Edge uses them as practical guidance when measurement is available.

Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)

2.5 s or less

How soon the main content of the page appears.

Interaction to Next Paint (INP)

200 ms or less

How quickly the page responds when someone taps or clicks.

Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)

0.1 or less

How much the layout jumps around while it loads.

What this tells us: these are performance targets measured at the 75th percentile of page loads, segmented across mobile and desktop, using real-user (field) data.

What it does not tell us: that meeting them will increase enquiries, bookings or sales. Those depend on many other things. A new site with few visitors may not have enough field data to measure at all, in which case lab tests are used as a rough guide only.

Source: Google, web.dev: Web Vitals. Thresholds checked October 2026.
